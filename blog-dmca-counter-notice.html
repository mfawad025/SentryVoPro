What Is a DMCA Counter-Notice and What Should You Do?

A counter-notice is a normal part of the process. It starts a clock, and what you do in the next two weeks matters.

What a counter-notice is

When a platform removes content after your takedown notice, the person who posted it can reply with a counter-notice. In it they say, under penalty of perjury, that the content was removed by mistake or misidentification. They also give their name and address and agree that a court can hear the dispute.

A counter-notice does not decide who is right. It moves the dispute from the platform to the two of you.

What happens to the content

Under US law, once the platform gets a valid counter-notice it passes it to you. If you do not tell the platform that you have started a court case, it may put the content back online between 10 and 14 business days after it received the counter-notice. If you do start a case and tell the platform in time, the content generally stays down while the case goes on. Platforms apply this in their own way, so read the message they send you.

What you can do

  1. Re-check your claim. Look at the page again. Did you give permission before? Was the post a short review or comment that might count as fair use? Was the content someone else's work?
  2. Withdraw if you made a mistake. Telling the platform that you withdraw your notice is better than letting a wrong claim go to court.
  3. Talk to a copyright lawyer if the content matters to you and the poster is clearly copying your work. Starting a court case costs money and takes time, so decide with advice.
  4. Report the next copy. If the content comes back, the same notice process starts again, and you can report to the host and to search engines as well.

Things to avoid

  • Do not contact the poster with threats. Keep communication in writing and in the platform's process.
  • Do not share the poster's name and address that came in the counter-notice. It is given to you for the dispute, not to be published.
  • Do not send a new notice for the same page if you are not sure the claim is right.

Keep records

Save your original file with its date, your first notice, the platform's reply and the counter-notice. If this ever goes to court, these records decide the case.

This guide is general information, not legal advice. Counter-notice rules for sites outside the US differ. A lawyer can tell you what applies to your situation.
