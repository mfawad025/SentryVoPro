How to Find Where Your Content Has Been Leaked

You cannot report what you cannot find. These are the searches that find most copies, and how to keep track of what you have found.

Start with your name

Use quotation marks so Google looks for the exact phrase, and try the spellings fans use. For a creator called "Jane Doe" with the username "janedoe", search for each of these separately:

  • "jane doe" and "janedoe"
  • "jane doe" leaked and "janedoe" leaked
  • "jane_doe" and "jane-doe" because many sites write names this way in the address

Check the second page of results as well. Copies are often on pages that rank below the first few results.

Search by site

To look inside one website, add site: and the domain. For example "janedoe" site:t.me searches Telegram's public pages. Do the same for forums and file-sharing sites you have already seen your name on.

Search by picture

If a photo of yours has been copied, a reverse image search can find where else it appears. Upload the original to Google Images or Google Lens and look through the results. Use your original file rather than a cropped or compressed copy, because it gives better matches. Pictures that look similar but are not yours will appear too, so check each one.

Know which results are worth reporting

  • A page that shows your work: report it. Copy the full address of that page.
  • A site's home page or search page: not the content itself. Open it, find the page that holds your work and report that address.
  • A page that only mentions your name: this is usually not copyright infringement. Do not report it as if it were.
  • Your own pages and official accounts: never report these.

Keep a record

Use a simple spreadsheet with these columns: the full address, the date you found it, a short note on what it shows, the date you reported it, and whether it is still online a week later. This list is what you hand to a helper, an agency or a takedown service, and it lets you see whether reports are working.

Set a routine

Search once a week and repeat the same queries so you can compare results. Add Google Alerts for your name and username for new pages. Expect to repeat this, because new copies appear after old ones are removed.
