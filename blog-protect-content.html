5 Ways to Protect Your Content Before It Gets Stolen

None of these guarantee your content never leaks — nothing does. But each one makes leaks easier to prove, trace, or catch early.

1. Watermark consistently, not just occasionally

A visible or subtle watermark on every piece of paid content makes reposted copies instantly identifiable as stolen, and makes your ownership obvious to a host reviewing a takedown request. Consistency matters more than the specific style — a watermark you only use sometimes is easy for buyers to learn to avoid or crop out.

2. Keep dated originals somewhere separate from where you publish

Store your original files (with metadata/timestamps intact) somewhere separate from your public posting platform. If a dispute ever arises about who owns a piece of content, having the original file with an earlier timestamp than any leaked copy is straightforward proof.

3. Review your platform's download/screenshot protections

Most subscription platforms have some settings around screenshot warnings or download restrictions — they won't stop a determined person, but they raise the effort required, which measurably reduces casual reposting.

4. Track where you post, and keep that list updated

Keep a running list of every platform and account where you post official content — this matters more than people expect. It's exactly what separates your own official pages from leaked copies when something automated (or a person doing a manual search) is trying to tell the two apart. Update it whenever you add a new platform or account.

5. Set up ongoing monitoring instead of occasional manual searches

A one-time search for your name catches what's already indexed today — it says nothing about what gets posted next week. Leaks tend to surface gradually, and the gap between "leaked" and "noticed" is usually where the most damage happens. Ongoing, scheduled scanning closes that gap in a way manual searching never quite keeps up with.
